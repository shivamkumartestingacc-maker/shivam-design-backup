01The problem

Random rewards make users stop caring.

Currently, users win a scratch card after some eligible payments. The reward inside is completely random, and it's often an offer that isn't valuable to them. When users keep getting rewards that are irrelevant and of no value, they stop caring about rewards altogether.

What's hurting the current experience

01

Irrelevant rewards

A coupon or scratch card offer of no value to the user.

02

Uncertain value

The value of a scratch card stays hidden until it's scratched.

03

No control

Nothing they do changes what they get.

04

Ignored

After enough misses, they stop opening cards.

Business goal

Get users to make their next payment with Paytm UPI, and increase their loyalty to the app.

User problem

Rewards are random, so they often feel useless, and users have no control over them.

04The idea

From “pay and hope” to “pay and choose”.

Instead of randomly assigning scratch cards whose value may be irrelevant, let users choose the rewards that are useful to them and then redeem them. Rewards stay valuable for users, and the redemption rate goes up.

07Measuring success

What I'd track if this shipped.

This is a concept, so these are the numbers I would track if it launched.

Payment completionGuardrail. Rewards must never slow down paying.→ Hold
What comes next: variable rewards

Once these metrics show that users pick and redeem their rewards, we can bring in a variable reward system. The variability won't be in the reward itself, which users still choose. It will be in the progress towards unlocking it, or claiming it again.

08Reflection

What I learned.

01

Choice only works if the rewards are good. Picking from three bad rewards doesn't help.

02

Showing progress, like “1 of 2 payments done”, gives users a reason to make the next payment.

03

The prototype shows how the flow works. Only a real test with users can show if they pay more often, and how much choosing their own reward changes their payment habit.

What the business can do

Work with brands that users actually want. Better offers mean more rewards get used, which raises the redemption rate, a key business metric.

Documentation

Research and process

01Why Paytm RewardsA reward moment that already exists but isn't working.

Most UPI apps work the same way, so rewards are one of the few ways to stand out. Paytm already shows a scratch card after some payments. I wanted to see if better design alone, without spending more on rewards, could make that card useful again, and build users' loyalty to the app.

AARRR stageRetention

Out of scopeMerchant partnerships, reward budgets, incentive economics

04Affinity clustersFour patterns from the raw notes.
Rewards stopped feeling like rewards
  • “I used to open every scratch card because I thought I might get something good.”
  • “I honestly don't remember the last one.”
  • “After getting those repeatedly, I stopped opening them.”
→ Excitement, then disappointment, then indifference.
Guaranteed value beats uncertainty
  • “Cashback. I know I'll definitely use it.”
  • “With Kiwi I know what benefit I'm getting.”
  • “Random coupons don't really help me.”
→ “Will this be useful?” beats “can this surprise me?”
People want to own the outcome
  • “I'd rather choose what I'm working toward.”
  • “If every payment got me closer to something I actually wanted, I'd probably keep checking it.”
→ Payment → progress → the reward they wanted.
Rewards attract, product value retains
  • “I used to switch between Paytm and Google Pay depending on offers.”
  • “Most of my friends are on PhonePe, so splitting bills is easier.”
→ Rewards drive trial. Habit keeps people.
05Insights → opportunitiesWhat each pattern means for the design.
InsightTensionOpportunity
Irrelevant rewards erode trustPeople want every payment to feel rewarding, but irrelevant rewards taught them rewards aren't worth a look.Rebuild confidence that rewards are worth opening.
Predictable value winsSurprise is exciting, but when it keeps missing, certainty is worth more.Make the value clear before they commit.
No control, no motivationPeople will work for rewards, but not when they can't shape the outcome.Let them choose, and show progress towards it.

09Open questionsWhat isn't validated yet.
KnownFive conversations and a working end-to-end prototype.
Not validatedWhen users move to the habit stage, progress per payment, reward economics, retention impact.
